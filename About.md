Living Blood — About

Living Blood may resemble ordinary blood, but it is a living and highly intelligent organism.

Although it looks like blood, its presence can sometimes be detected by observing its movement. It may move in ways that are either visible or invisible to the human eye.

Living Blood can consume parts of a host's body and use the host as a means of survival and reproduction.

The first known human infection occurred when a person consumed the meat of an infected deer.

The deer had developed altered blood after consuming radioactive grass. This caused the Living Blood to eventually enter the human body through the infected meat.

Once inside a human host, Living Blood begins spreading and reproducing throughout the body.

Its true nature remained unknown for a long time because researchers initially believed it was simply a strange virus.

Living Blood is not an ordinary virus.

It is a living organism capable of controlling its host, reproducing itself, and searching for new hosts.

When frozen at −10°C, Living Blood does not die. Instead, it enters a dormant state and remains inactive until conditions allow it to become active again.
